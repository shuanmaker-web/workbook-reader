@passage
When one thinks of sugar cane, human rather than mechanical fuel probably comes more readily to mind. A single thirty-gram serving of pure sugar cane juice delivers around a hundred calories. It also provides over twenty grams' worth of carbohydrates, those reviving organic compounds living tissues use to store energy.

[[1]] the majority of consumers with a sweet tooth might not realize, however, is that sugar cane has the capacity to fuel artificial objects. By crushing sugar cane stalks, the energy-rich internal juices can be extracted and collected. [[2]], a refined liquid is combined with a family of single-celled organisms known as Saccharomyces cerevisiae yeast.

These industrious microorganisms break down the sticky substance on a chemical level, generating bubbles and heat akin to the process observed in the preparation of beer and other [[3]]. This warm, bubbly liquid is colloquially referred to as "beer" due to its alcohol content, ranging from five to twelve percent ethanol.

Following two additional purifying processes, the result is 100% ethanol—a potent "biofuel" or biological fuel [[4]] powering vehicles of various types. Moreover, it can be blended with conventional petroleum, gasoline, and diesel, producing environmentally friendlier fuel [[5]]. Who knew a plant could double as a tasty treat and fuel for your car? Sugar cane is truly a jack of all trades!

Children are supposed to be full of energy. Sadly, a lot of that abundant inner drive is nowadays being wasted on electronic devices and computerized games. In countless schools and parks, play areas [[6]] and sports fields are growing silent.

Playground Energy is a company on a mission to arrest this worrying trend. Its founders are just as determined to get kids to once again be their old active selves. With projects scattered across Europe, and a(n) [[7]] in both Australia and the US, they're playing an increasingly important part worldwide.

Playground Energy creates all the usual playground attractions—swings, seesaws, rocking horses, etc. But they add a clever and energizing twist. Equipped with special generators, its [[8]] are able to convert kinetic energy, the energy of movement, into electrical and other forms. [[9]] playing children set one of these swaying, spinning, or swinging pieces of equipment into motion, they can power various applications set up around the play area. Simply by having good old-fashioned, energy-draining physical fun, they can light up multi-colored lights or toys. They can also produce fun, pre-recorded sound effects. Their activities can even be [[10]] powering music-playing Bluetooth speakers.

As one might expect, such eye-catching and exciting features are a great motivation for kinetic kids to move even more!

The drama began in the early hours of July 30 with the tripping of a mainline "circuit breaker," which is a device that can safely [[12]] an excessively large electric current. Damage to the circuit and equipment it passes through can then be [[13]].

In this particular case, however, the circuit breaker [[14]] its task a little too well. It triggered a series of similar interruptions at a power station where the line was connected. This, in turn, provoked a whole chain of pauses in power in other lines.

This then spilled over into neighboring states and [[15]] the failings of several ill-equipped backup systems. Before they knew what had struck them, a staggering 230 million people across northern and eastern India found themselves the [[16]] of a massive blackout. They also found themselves without a single spark of electricity.

Homes, water treatment plants and electric pumps, hospitals, trains, and even airports without [[17]] to backup generators were soon in the same boat. By morning, a quarter of the country's [[18]] were back in the pre-power 19th century and a frantic fifteen hours were spent just to regain 80% capacity.

Sad to say, the next day was even worse. A faulty electric switch downed multiple recovering power stations once again at around lunchtime, eventually impacting the electricity [[19]] in 22 of India's 28 states.

This time, a shocking 600 million people—half of the world's population and nearly 10% of the world's—were left powerless. @passage
Around a quarter of everything in the known universe is made up of its most abundant element: hydrogen. It's rather plentiful down here on Earth too. Hence, you'll find its atoms in virtually all earthly things: plants, animals, and water. It's also present, of course, in the other important "h": humans.

[[21]] The reason this is a pity is that hydrogen gas is a fantastically clean renewable energy alternative to fossil fuels. Electricity can be used very efficiently to obtain hydrogen from water with no harmful byproducts. Secondly, no world-warming CO2 is produced when hydrogen is burned to create energy. When utilized in fuel cells, in fact, the only "extra" formed in combustion (burning), is good old H2O.

Germany, South Korea, China, Japan, and America are all well aware of these benefits. [[22]] China, in particular, is buying into this green technology in a big way and already has over ten thousand hydrogen-powered cars and trucks on its roads. Relying on many more of these, as well as electric vehicles, in years to come, they hope to have achieved net-zero carbon emissions for everything on wheels by 2050.

Australia, similarly, is making substantial investments in solar-powered hydrogen harnessing devices. [[23]] Functioning prototypes are already doing just that, and a ten-square-meter unit, they claim, will be all that's needed to fuel home cooking and heating.

[[24]] With research, innovation, and a bit of luck, this truly natural gas will hopefully be powering a lot more for us in the future.
@end
