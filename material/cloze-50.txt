The marigold is a group of over 50 related flowering plants in the Tagetes genus. The most popular [[6]] are known for their vibrant, warm-colored flowers that bloom in large, showy, pom-pom-like shapes. Their petals are often brilliant shades of orange, red, or yellow with delicate, lacy frills that can brighten anyone's day. As a result of such [[7]], marigolds are extremely popular annuals frequently seen in flower markets and florists worldwide.

As annual plants, marigolds complete their entire lifecycle within a single growing season, from seed germination to full maturity with vibrant blossoms and then to seed production. Their existence [[8]] just from spring through summer. Although marigold plants are short-lived, they persist through self-seeding, allowing [[9]] generations to emerge seasonally year after year.

Marigolds belong to the same "genus," a classification between "species" and "family." The name "Tagetes" is derived from a mythical Roman prophet who was believed to have emerged spontaneously from the plowed earth. This legend likely inspired the genus name [[10]] marigolds seem to grow effortlessly across fields and gardens season after season, much like the mythical Tages himself. Thus, marigolds are associated with this tale due to their resilience and recurring seasonal blooms.

@passage
Kenzie Kizer was busy ridding her backyard of weeds one fine spring day, a task many homeowners undertake, when she was suddenly interrupted in her outdoor labors. Without warning, she experienced a severe burning sensation in her arms. [[21]] Thinking some tiny fragments of a stem, branch, or thorn had merely pierced her skin and caused all the irritation, she wasn't too alarmed. Although her discomfort was rather intense, she was confident that putting down her tools and taking a cooling shower would easily remedy matters.

The cooling shower did bring some relief, but this measure was not enough to prevent what happened next. [[22]] For one, she felt extremely unsteady on her feet and was even shaking. Her tongue too was visibly swollen and the heat in her skin had returned with greater vigor than before.

The situation became critical when Kenzie started experiencing difficulty breathing. At this point, she sought treatment at a nearby hospital emergency room. [[23]] It turns out that during her gardening endeavors, Kenzie had come into contact with the stems, leaves, or roots of "poison hemlock."

Poison hemlock is quite common in the state where Kenzie resides. It has small, white umbrella-like clusters of flowers, and stems spotted with red or purple spots and patches. [[24]] If swallowed, however, poison hemlock can kill a healthy adult in under three hours.
@end
