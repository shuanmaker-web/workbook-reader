@passage
When coming face to face with poverty, it is perhaps fair to say that many people turn a blind eye. Should we encounter [[1]] on the street, therefore, this may not lead to any meaningful interaction, or to a genuine attempt to improve their struggle-ridden lives.

Too often, in other words, the poor are seen as a necessary evil in the society. Seldom, however, [[2]] its far more privileged citizens feel directly responsible for assisting them.

Luckily, every now and then, special individuals come along who do not simply stick their heads in the sand. [[3]] pretending not to observe poverty, or simply choosing to dismiss it from their minds, these people will sit down and give considerable thought to changing things for the better.

One such person is Kacey Wong. A resident of Hong Kong, Kacey set about designing public display units or statues that could be [[4]] sleeping shelters for those who do not have a home.

His most famous design is a metal robot sculpture that can, in just a few simple steps, be "transformed" into a compact living space for whoever needs it. The robot can be folded out into a full-length, covered bed, or [[5]] into a chair and table.

For those with nowhere to turn, this innovative street art is a precious piece of home.

Certain countries, for instance, only permit multiple marriages if these are prescribed by a man's faith or recognized local customs. Other societies only welcome such arrangements if the husband's first wife gives her [[7]] to any additional unions.

With more potential breadwinners under one roof, they claim, enlarged clans also have a better chance of enjoying [[9]].

Another motivation for supporting such a practice, they maintain, is that it significantly eases the burden of bearing and rearing children. Additional wives, moreover, can help to perform these motherly requirements, and even allow earlier spouses [[10]] alternative careers.

@passage
These days, the adjective "smart" is being paired with a growing number of nouns. Smartphones and smartwatches are everywhere, and it's not unheard of to have smart TVs and smart fridges [[11]] at home. In the not-too-distant future, we might all be living in "smart societies" as well.

In a smart society, the most is made of digital technology to create a(n) [[12]] living space that's more energy efficient, safer, more convenient, and more pleasant to stay in.

Individual housing units in sophisticated residential communities such as these are all interconnected. Their various smart devices, appliances, and energy systems are linked to a(n) [[13]] database. This allows the community to constantly monitor all its various components in real time. It's thus able to [[14]] extensive data with regard to water and energy usage patterns.

With all this enlightening digital information at their fingertips, management and residents can then [[15]] living arrangements accordingly. They can also fine-tune device and appliance settings in order to maximize efficiency and [[16]] waste.

At the same time, renewable energy generated from eco-friendly fittings such as solar paneling can be evenly distributed. So too, can water [[17]] from smart rainwater filtering systems. Pooling resources in this fashion utilizes stored-up energy and water reserves to the full. It also [[18]] individual units from unnecessarily tapping into less sustainable supplies.

Last but by no means least, a smart society also connects member citizens. It [[19]] this goal with high-tech digital platforms through which to communicate and collaborate. Since everyone can offer feedback and work together for the benefit of the entire community, everyone remains engaged and informed.

Everybody is thus included when it comes to making [[20]] improvements and adjustments. Everyone, in other words, is involved in keeping the society smart.

@passage
Vicious rumors were going around that foreigners were coming in and "stealing" locals' jobs. As these false reports circulated, public sentiment seemed to swing heavily in favor of more conservative domestic labor policies. [[21]] By having outsiders enter the country and occupy professional positions, the government was "plainly letting them down."

Soon, locals were angered by the mere presence of immigrants and other perfectly legal visitors. They took to the streets and openly protested against permitting immigration of any kind. People considered insufficiently localized were physically attacked, and their property was targeted. This led to injuries and damage. [[22]]

The scenario described above is a classic example of "xenophobia." This is the prejudiced tendency to distrust, fear, dislike, or even hate people belonging to a different country, territory, tribe, culture, or religion.

[[23]] It breaks up communities and can result in highly destructive acts of intimidation and violence. In the long run, this type of discrimination can, moreover, lead to a lack of diversity and a decrease in foreign interest and investment.

Fortunately, there are ways to counter xenophobic behavior and trends. [[24]] So too are initiatives and activities that bring together different members of the community, or try to include and absorb groups perceived as "outsiders." Finally, simply speaking out against xenophobia can spark constructive public debate and help unite rather than divide.
@end
