@passage
Contact sports can be hard on the human body, leading to chipped teeth, twisted ankles, black eyes, muscle tears, cuts, bruises, and broken bones. The list of hits and hurts goes on and on.

In the most physical sports, like boxing, rugby, wrestling, and American football, the risk of [[11]] a head injury like "concussion" is also relatively high. When concussed due to a violent blow to the head, a player will experience a period of light-headedness or confusion. This could be [[12]] by blurred vision and a ringing sensation in the ears.

Although such symptoms tend not to last long, they can be followed by [[13]] of headaches, weakness, and fatigue. Patients with concussions often experience vomiting and may respond more slowly to questions and instructions than they [[14]] would.

Thankfully, these unpleasant side-effects are [[15]] and merely persist for a few hours, after which the battered, battle-hardened player usually makes a full recovery. However, the danger arises when athletes, especially children, suffer concussions and then continue playing. Repeated injuries can lead to serious and even [[16]] brain damage, and must be avoided at all costs.

This is where high-tech gum shields or mouth guards come in. Traditionally designed to safeguard players' smiles and reduce the impact of falls and collisions, the latest versions of this dental protective [[17]] take safety a step further. Equipped with state-of-the-art sensors, they can [[18]] measure hits to the head. They then change color according to the force behind these collisions—blue for medium force and red for hardest blows. This serves as a clear signal that the player should be examined for concussion or immediately [[19]] to avoid risk.

So the color-changing technology provides a timely warning system, [[20]] users to exercise caution after hard head contacts. This innovation allows athletes, coaches, and teammates to better prevent severe injuries resulting from repeated concussions during contact sports.
@end
