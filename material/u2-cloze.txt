@passage
A paperclip, made of steel wire bent into a hooped shape, is an instrument used to hold sheets of paper together. This common [[1]] is a wonder of simplicity and function. But where did this simple, cheap, and indispensable invention come from?

In the late 19th century, the most common way to hold papers together was by using a pin. Although the pin was an inexpensive tool and was easily [[2]], it would leave holes in the paper. Later, as steel wire became more common, inventors began to notice its elastic feature. With this feature, it could be stretched and [[3]] various clip-like objects. In the years just prior to 1900, quite a few paperclip designs emerged. The name most frequently [[4]] the paperclip invention is Johan Vaaler, a Norwegian inventor. However, Vaaler's clips were not the same as the paperclips currently in use. Specifically, they did not have the interior loop we see today. The [[5]] looped design was invented by Gem Manufacturing Ltd. in England. This clip is therefore sometimes [[6]] the Gem clip.

Because of Vaaler, the paperclip played an important [[7]] role in Norway. During World War II, Norway was occupied by the Nazis. Norwegians were prohibited from wearing any [[8]] of their national unity, such as buttons with the initials of their king. Thus, in [[9]], they started wearing paperclips to show their solidarity. The reason for doing this was simple: Paperclips were a Norwegian invention whose original function was to bind together. After the war, a giant paperclip statue was erected in Oslo to [[10]] Vaaler—even though his design was never actually manufactured.
@end

Q 1 | bank | 104學測

= C
~ common 是形容詞，空格應為名詞，依文意可判斷答案為 (C) device 設備、裝置。
~文意 這個常見的裝置，是個設計簡單、功能性高的驚奇發明。

Q 2 | bank | 104學測
stem: （第 2 格）
= D
~ easily 是副詞，空格應為形容詞或被動語態所需的動詞 (pp)；除了依文意可判斷答案為 (D) removable 可移動的。*選項 known as、twisted into 跟 associated with 後面都必須有受詞。
~文意 雖然別針是個不昂貴的工具，也很容易取下來，不過總是會在紙張上留下一個洞。

Q 3 | bank | 104學測
stem: （第 3 格）
= H
~ and（對等連接詞）前後字彙詞性要一致，空格前面是被動語態 be stretched，所以空格應和 stretched 一樣為動詞 (pp)；依文意可判斷答案為 (H) twisted into 被捻成。
~文意 因為有彈性，可以彎折，也可以捻成各種夾子的形狀。

Q 4 | bank | 104學測

= I
~ frequently 是副詞，paperclip invention 是名詞，空格應該是形容詞；由於本句主詞是 the name，動詞是 is，可見中間所有字都是在修飾 name，再依文意可判斷答案為當形容詞用的動詞 (pp)：(I) associated with 與…有關聯的、被提及的。
~文意 迴紋針的發明，最常被提及的名字就是 Johan Vaaler，一位挪威發明家。

@passage
Solar panels, often seen on rooftops or across vast fields, are a sight increasingly familiar in our modern landscape. These sleek, shiny rectangles, typically composed of photovoltaic cells, [[11]] a patchwork of glossy tiles when installed. Their appearance [[12]], sometimes appearing as uniform arrays, while other times they are scattered across surfaces randomly. Regardless of their layout, their purpose remains clear: to make use of the sun's energy and [[13]] it into usable electricity.

Functioning as miniature power plants atop our homes or in expansive solar farms, solar panels operate through a fascinating [[14]] known as the photovoltaic effect. When sunlight strikes the surface of these panels, they will [[15]] photons, or particles of light, and then release electrons from the atoms within the semiconductor material. Afterward, the direct current (DC) electricity [[16]] is changed into alternating current (AC) through an inverter for use in households.

The [[17]] of solar panels brings with it advantages and disadvantages. On the positive side, they provide a clean, renewable source of energy, reducing [[18]] on fossil fuels and reducing greenhouse gas emissions. However, their production involves some environmental [[19]]. The treatment of waste water during production, for instance, is a costly and troublesome matter. The prices [[20]] for installation may also discourage regular homeowners from such an idea. That being said, improvements in technology and government incentives are making solar power more accessible and affordable over time.
