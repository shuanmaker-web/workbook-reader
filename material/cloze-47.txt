@group 一、綜合測驗

@passage
Ever glanced down at your phone and wondered what the "G" in 4G or 5G stands for? The answer is "Generation." A 5G mobile network is thus a fifth generation or version which has been [[1]] by four similar systems.

As one might expect from this step-up in cellular technology, it aims to make phone and online services more reliable. It also promises to boost the speed of data transmission by reducing "latency." Latency refers to the delay in the transfer of information, measured in milliseconds, across mobile networks. Online gamers often [[2]] many missed targets [[2]] this type of delay. Similarly, anyone streaming a concert or sporting event on their smart devices "in real time" might notice that the [[3]] are actually lagging slightly behind the live action. This difference can often make the viewing experience less fluid and enjoyable.

The degree [[4]] latency can be lowered depends on how rapidly data can be transmitted to the receiver. Since the 5G of the future will carry more gigabits per second (Gbps) than ever before, this could greatly reduce delay by ten times. Accelerating information transfer by this [[5]] won't just make for a better streaming or gaming experience. It could also make the remote operation of engineering or medical instruments safer and more effective, and even save lives.

@passage
It made quite a splash in computing circles when one of the world's leading laptop producers introduced their all new "butterfly keyboard" in 2015. This feature allowed them to create slim, lightweight notebook computers that were more [[11]] than ever before, fitting easily in a handbag.

On the surface, it promised to be an industry "game changer," but it didn't take long for the innovation to take a heavy [[12]]. Unfortunately, in favoring [[13]] over function to such an unprecedented degree, the company had lost sight of the overall user experience. The keyboard's keys turned out to be so thin that customers soon complained that the distinct lack of "travel" cushioning was not very [[14]] to the touch.

The "travel" of a computer key is the distance it moves down to reach its activation point when pressed. After the manufacturer had slashed this down to the [[15]] minimum of one millimeter, it didn't take long for everyone to put their finger on the problem. To make matters worse, typing was now found to be a far louder activity than before. Thinner keys, it seems, made for noisier devices and this too had a negative impact on consumer [[16]] levels across the board. When new models came out the following year, "butterfly" keys were reported to be sticking or not responding at all when [[17]].

Four years of complaints, repairs, maintenance, and redesign later, the tech giant finally [[18]] their old yet trusty "scissor switch" keyboards. The butterfly's wings had finally been [[19]], and fans welcomed the return of quieter, more durable keys.

So which company was it that was responsible for this key product [[20]]? Well, let's just say that if they hadn't shelved the idea pretty quickly, it could well have been a bad apple. Fortunately for them, butterfly keyboards never quite took off.
@end

It's long been assumed that high-tech devices and the beach simply don't mix. Holiday hotspots, after all, hold plenty of perils for such instruments. Despite precautions, sand mysteriously worms its way into electrical ports and swiftly scratches screens. [[21]] Exposure to salty seawater is especially notorious for hampering technological performance, and depths of merely a meter inevitably seal the fate of anything smart. In addition, it can cause harm to the internal components of devices, causing problems like rust and malfunctions.

Fortunately, this is not the case anymore. [[22]] These incredible gadgets can also remain a full six meters underwater for half an hour, without coming to any harm. The trend toward beach-friendly tech is also extending to other electronic essentials. The latest Bluetooth speakers are waterproof up to one meter for thirty minutes and boast a battery life that can keep the party going for twenty hours. Modern earphones allow users to plunge in for a salty swim without any phone or stereo. [[23]] Apart from the above devices, there's also the underwater camera available. It operates up to 10 meters deep and has a durable battery, allowing around 70 minutes of continuous recording. Users have successfully used this camera for snorkeling, kayaking, and water rides at amusement parks.

In conclusion, technology has significantly evolved to overcome the challenges of beach environments. [[24]] These innovations not only enrich our experiences but also broaden the scope of using electronics, making the beach more accessible and enjoyable for tech lovers.
