@passage
Turmeric has been astonishing people in the health and wellness world lately. You might have come into contact with this exotic spice in the supermarket and be wondering what all the fuss is about.

A cousin of ginger, turmeric is a golden-colored root with a slightly bitter [[6]] that Indians have used as a cooking spice, as well as a medicine, for thousands of years. The vapor that came from frying turmeric was once used to [[7]] blocked noses. Turmeric juice was also massaged into wounds to aid healing, and turmeric paste was applied to treat skin conditions. While critics might dismiss these usages as mere ancient superstition, recent studies have affirmed that turmeric really does have some incredible medicinal [[8]].

Turmeric's singular health benefits are [[9]] from a chemical called curcumin. This substance gives turmeric its [[10]] yellow color. Curcumin has been shown to help with a(n) [[11]] of medical issues, from sleep disturbances, to mild infections, to more acute diseases like diabetes, depression, and cancer. No [[12]] ancient Indians referred to turmeric as jayanti, meaning "one who is victorious over diseases."

Today, thanks to the growing realization that curcumin can [[13]] healing, people are incorporating turmeric into their lives in numerous ways. Formulas for making turmeric-based beauty products abound on the Internet. The spice is also becoming a(n) [[14]] addition to people's diets and is often added to soups, teas, and even pancakes.

Unfortunately, even if you [[15]] a lot of turmeric, there will be little curcumin in your bloodstream, as our bodies don't absorb it well. However, combining turmeric with a source of fat can significantly increase the absorption of curcumin. That's why many people drink turmeric milk, a healthy alternative to coffee.
@end

@passage
Bullying has long been a miserable fact of life that used to be limited to schools and schoolyards. In the digital age, however, a new form of bullying has emerged: cyberbullying—bullying using the Internet. Young people are connected twenty-four hours a day through text messaging, e-mail, and social media. [[16]]

There are many examples of cyberbullying. Someone might browse someone else's social media account to find, or post, embarrassing pictures of that person. It could also involve spreading rumors online or sending mean messages. Cyberbullies often keep their identities hidden, so you may not know who is bullying you. No matter the means, cyberbullying can leave people feeling hurt or depressed. That's why it's important to know what to do if you or someone you care about is bullied online. [[17]] Bullies often feed off people's reactions. Another helpful tip is to take a screenshot as proof. That way, cyberbullies won't be able to refute their actions later.

Perhaps the best way to curb cyberbullying is to prevent it from happening in the first place. It often occurs when someone takes control of one of your social media accounts. Keep your devices locked, and avoid sharing passwords, even with your closest friends. [[18]] This lets you choose who can see certain information. Additionally, remember that the Internet is a public place. Even if you remove a post, someone else might have saved it.

[[19]] Still, you can take steps to fight it. If your school doesn't have any guidelines to tackle cyberbullying, point this out. If one of your friends is being bullied, stand up for them. Or, if you know a cyberbully, call them out on it. Doing so could save someone a lot of distress.
@end

@passage
Traveling in Europe may cost a fortune, but it's definitely worth it. Even a short visit will allow you to take in multiple countries, cultures, landmarks, and food. Getting around is quite easy and doesn't take much effort. Europe's excellent transportation and the "Schengen Agreement" mean that you can cross borders really easily with a single visa in your passport. Buying a European rail pass will not only save you money, but will enable you to enjoy outstanding landscapes as you travel through snow-capped mountains, green fields, and glorious coastlines that will take your breath away.

Many people visit Europe to absorb its rich history. If you enjoy traveling back in time, you should spend some time visiting the famous ruins of the Acropolis in Athens, Greece. But why stop there? History surrounds you almost everywhere you go, from medieval castles in England and Scotland, to cathedrals in Spain, to France's famous palaces.

As you travel through Europe, you will encounter so much culture that it will be hard to take it all in. London, Paris, and Amsterdam are just three European cities that are home to famous museums and impressive art galleries. If music is more your thing, don't hesitate to stop by Vienna, Austria. With so much to see and do, you'll soon realize that one visit can never be enough!

@group 五、混合題

@passage
Apparently we humans are the most intelligent species on the planet, but it turns out that many of our ideas about other animals are mistaken. Here are some commonly-held beliefs about animals that simply aren't true.

You can't teach an old dog new tricks, can you? Most dogs are perfectly capable of learning at any age. It takes about 15 minutes a day for two weeks to train your dog to do simple tasks like sitting or fetching. While a newborn puppy might be too young, an old dog in good shape shouldn't have a problem. Touching a toad can give you warts, can't it? It's true that warts are contagious, but you won't get them from toads. These creatures, which are small animals similar to frogs, often have bumps on their skin that resemble warts. It could be what inspired this myth. Bats cannot see, can they? This is a very commonly believed myth, but while many bats use echoes to assist them in flight, they also rely on vision. A camel carries water in its hump, doesn't it? Not so! A camel's hump is filled with fat. If the fat were distributed around its body, a camel's body heat would be uncomfortably high in the hot desert. It is true, however, that camels can go up to seven months without water.

It might be easy to get the wrong idea about animals that we don't come across very frequently. However, you don't need to be an animal expert to separate the truth from the myths. If you want to be as wise as an owl, just check the facts!
@end
