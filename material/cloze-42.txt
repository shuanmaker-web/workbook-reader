Once they'd identified a metal element with a nucleus easy to split up, the next step was obvious. All they had to do now was [[6]] a neutron, that component of an atom that doesn't possess a charge, at it. They did so and, to their delight, found that the nucleus did indeed divide into two new ones. [[7]] this pair of wholly separate nuclei, they also had two or three supplementary neutrons.

It had been a very productive collision but this was merely the beginning. The additional created neutrons then started [[8]] other nuclei of the element. In a fraction of a second, a lively [[9]] of invading neutrons and multiplying nuclei had been started. Neutron rockets were flying everywhere and nuclei under siege were doubling in the blink of an eye.

All the while, two bonus byproducts of this string of collisions and divisions were also being generated: heat and radiation, both of which could be used to warm up a cooling agent to produce steam. From this point on, it was merely a case of directing this warm vapor at rapidly turning machines which would see to it that generators were [[10]] to produce electricity. And that is how, in short, nuclear energy was born.

When cows eat and drink, certain natural internal processes are bound to take their course. As a result, a(n) [[11]] proportion of this consumed liquid and digested material is converted into edible steaks and the thick, creamy liquid we drizzle over our cereals.

Apart from meat and milk, these [[12]] workings inevitably produce a significant amount of waste as well. While this cow poo or "manure" can be used as natural [[13]], its creation does stink for a number of reasons. In addition to the [[14]] odor and the hours of labor and energy required to process and transport this organic waste, cow droppings release huge volumes of harmful gas into the atmosphere. Finding a more sustainable means of [[15]] of this manure, therefore, would have a huge positive environmental impact.

Bloom Energy Corporation has not only realized this, but even gone a step further. By [[16]] cutting-edge fuel cells to harness and store the energy released by biogas, the company has turned a dairy farm in California into an impressive self-reliant and eco-friendly operation.

How it works is that waste water [[17]] pee and poo is drained away from the living areas of the farm's almost 7,000 cows. It is then piped to a large, covered area known as a digester. As the organic matter breaks down in this waste warehouse, gas is released. This is captured by the digester's cover and fed into a plant that [[18]] it into clean, usable biogas.

The biogas is then passed over special fuel cells to create an electro-chemical process that [[19]] electricity. At present, the farm's biogas system generates enough electrical energy to power over 750 [[20]]. For all practical purposes, it is effectively able to power itself. Most importantly, virtually no carbon dioxide is being released in the process.

@passage
For centuries, humans inhabiting every corner of this planet have been burning fossil fuels. They've been doing so for heating and cooking and to generate electricity. Quite often, they have also done so with a view to getting from A to B. [[21]] That is to say, many of us are swiftly gravitating toward cleaner, greener ones.

In 2023, global investment in renewable technology overtook fossil fuel spending for the first time in human history. [[22]] This was around $0.7 trillion more than poured into projects centered around oil, coal, and other more traditional fuels.

China, for the moment, is leading this charge toward a more sustainable tomorrow. The Asian clean energy powerhouse is forecast to have doubled its capacity for producing solar and wind power by the time the sun sets in 2025. [[23]] This is especially impressive if one considers that they appear likely to accomplish it a full five years earlier than previously anticipated.

Though nowhere near on the same scale, the Brits are not lagging too far behind, proportionally. [[24]] What's more, its domestic and offshore renewable infrastructure will only expand in the future.

Lastly, even the United States is finally starting to ditch dirty fuels. America produced nearly a quarter of the power it consumed in 2023 with the aid of renewable sources. This marked an impressive one percent increase per year for the preceding decade. All signs are that a new day in the energy age is undoubtedly dawning.
